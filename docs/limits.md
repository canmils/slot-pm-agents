# What agents should not do

Read this before you build. These limits come from how slot games are made, regulated and sold.

## Never

1. **Approve game math.** RTP, volatility, max win and feature frequency must be simulated and signed off by a math engineer.
2. **Change a certified build.** Labs test a defined build and scope. Technical Compliance must assess changes against the applicable jurisdiction and process before release; do not assume either automatic acceptance or universal full recertification.
3. **Deploy to production.** Releases go through system testing, stage testing and operator activation, with people signing off.
4. **Give one revenue number for a new game.** Show comparables and a range.
5. **Store or send real player data** outside approved systems.
6. **Design features that push harmful play.** Every option must pass responsible gambling rules for the target markets.

## Be careful with

1. **Revenue comparisons between games of different ages.** Always compare at the same number of days after launch. A game live for 2 weeks and a game live for 2 years cannot be compared on total GGR.
2. **Small samples.** With 20 to 30 games, look for patterns, do not train a model.
3. **Exclusive launches.** A game live only with one operator for its first weeks reaches fewer players. Compare exclusives with exclusives.
4. **Seasonal games.** Christmas or Easter games peak and drop. Compare by season or exclude them.
5. **Competitor data.** Public signals (release dates, features) are reliable. Revenue estimates from third parties are not.

## Good fit

1. Summarising documents, reports and feedback.
2. Cost and timeline estimates from past projects.
3. Drafting briefs, stories and test cases for a person to review.
4. Post-launch reporting on data you already have.
5. Tracking public market and regulation changes.
